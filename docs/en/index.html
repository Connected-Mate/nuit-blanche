Questions

Your questions, answered

Does it really work in a bag? What about heat?

Yes, that’s what it’s for. Nuit Blanche watches your Mac’s heat signals: macOS’s thermal level and the battery temperature, every 20 seconds. When it gets warm (battery at 45 °C), it lets the screen sleep and slows Wi‑Fi scans, without stopping, and tells you: “Your Mac is getting warm”. Everything resumes once it cools down.

If it gets too hot (battery at 50 °C), it stops and normal sleep returns so your Mac can cool down: “Too hot: normal sleep restored to cool down.” It doesn’t restart on its own: you switch it back on once it has cooled. This protection is always on.

It’s a safety net, not a guarantee: the app can’t see your bag, it reacts to your Mac’s own signals. A bag that isn’t too tight is even better.

The train Wi‑Fi wants me to log in: what happens?

A Wi‑Fi that shows you a login page counts as “no internet”. After two failed checks (a single tunnel isn’t enough), your Mac joins your iPhone’s Personal Hotspot and stays on it while it works.

Does Personal Hotspot use my mobile data?

Yes: when your Mac goes online through your iPhone, it uses your mobile plan’s data, like any hotspot. That’s why, by default, Nuit Blanche only joins it when your Mac has no other way online. You can also tell it to always prefer it, or turn the option off.

Will it drain my battery?

An awake Mac uses more power than a sleeping one, that’s normal. On battery, below 10%, Nuit Blanche stops on its own, lets you know and lets your Mac sleep. In Options, you can pick 5 to 30%, or never. Plugged in, it never stops.

The window keeps you posted: “Awake for 1 h 20 · battery −18%”.

Is it safe for my Mac?

Yes. Nuit Blanche does one thing: it tells your Mac not to sleep while “Always awake” is on. As soon as you switch it off, quit the app or it gets too hot, your Mac gets its normal sleep back.

The app is notarized by Apple: Apple checked it before you can open it.

Why does it ask for permission the first time?

To stay awake with the lid closed, Nuit Blanche relies on a small helper in the background. Just once, macOS asks you to allow it, under “Allow in the Background”. One click on “Allow” opens the right setting, and a small panel points to the switch to turn on. After that, never again.

For Personal Hotspot, macOS also asks for Location access: that’s its rule for seeing Wi‑Fi network names. On a company Mac that blocks the helper, the app tells you plainly: lid-open only.

How does it update?

The app checks once a day and shows “Update available”. Nothing installs without your click.

Something wrong? How do I report it?

In Options, “Report a problem…” saves a small readable file on your Desktop (no network names, no emails) and opens a prefilled GitHub page. Nothing is sent on its own: you decide.

Which version of macOS do I need?

macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later, on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs.

How do I install and uninstall it?

Download the file, open it, and drag Nuit Blanche into your Applications folder.

To uninstall, just move the app to the Trash, even while it’s on: normal sleep comes back by itself within seconds. Quitting does the same.

Is it really free?

Yes. No subscription, no account, no ads, no tracking. Only the update check goes online, once a day.
